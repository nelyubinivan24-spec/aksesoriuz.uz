# Audience Strategy

## Purpose

Define the primary buyer groups, their motivations, objections, and preferred website paths.

## Audience Priority

Approved strategic direction:

The website should be universal for all qualified B2B clients. No major audience should be excluded at this stage.

Commercial emphasis should be guided by:

- market research;
- profitability;
- order size;
- repeat purchase potential;
- operational feasibility;
- category growth potential.

Working audience groups:

1. Restaurants and cafes.
2. Hotels and hospitality businesses.
3. Corporate and business clients.
4. Retail, packaging, and gift buyers.
5. Agencies, designers, and procurement intermediaries.
6. Other qualified custom-production buyers.

UX implication:

The site should give each major segment a clear path while keeping the homepage broad enough to communicate a universal production partner.

## Segment 1: Restaurants and Cafes

Likely needs:

- menu covers;
- bill folders and check holders;
- table tents;
- reserves;
- napkin holders;
- placemats;
- wooden tableware;
- branded service accessories.

Motivations:

- improve guest experience;
- make the venue feel more premium;
- create visual consistency;
- replace worn accessories;
- prepare a new opening or rebrand.

Objections:

- uncertainty about materials;
- unclear pricing;
- concerns about durability;
- timing before opening;
- quality of branding on small details.

Website path:

Homepage -> Restaurants page -> category/product -> request sample or quote.

Required proof:

- real restaurant products;
- material close-ups;
- durability explanation;
- production timelines;
- client examples.

## Segment 2: Hotels and Hospitality

Likely needs:

- room folders;
- guest information folders;
- key holders;
- branded trays;
- desk accessories;
- textile/uniform products;
- premium packaging.

Motivations:

- improve guest perception;
- standardize brand details;
- support premium room experience;
- order durable branded accessories.

Objections:

- quality consistency across quantity;
- material durability;
- ability to match brand guidelines;
- delivery and repeat orders.

Website path:

Homepage -> Hotels page -> hospitality catalog -> quote request.

Required proof:

- hotel product examples;
- batch consistency;
- brand matching options;
- repeat order process.

## Segment 3: Corporate and Business Clients

Likely needs:

- business sets;
- notebooks and planners;
- folders;
- cardholders;
- wallets;
- badges and certificates;
- corporate gifts.

Motivations:

- present a professional brand image;
- prepare gifts for partners or employees;
- support events and onboarding;
- create branded stationery.

Objections:

- budget uncertainty;
- minimum quantities;
- packaging options;
- premium feel of final product;
- deadline pressure.

Website path:

Homepage -> Business products -> product category -> quote request.

Required proof:

- gift set examples;
- packaging examples;
- branding methods;
- corporate client logos.

## Segment 4: Packaging and Souvenirs

Likely needs:

- branded boxes;
- souvenir packaging;
- custom product packaging;
- printed materials;
- finishing and decoration.

Motivations:

- make product presentation stronger;
- support retail sales;
- create memorable unboxing;
- align packaging with brand identity.

Objections:

- custom size feasibility;
- minimum order;
- material choice;
- color accuracy;
- production timeline.

Website path:

Homepage -> Packaging page -> examples -> quote request with dimensions and quantity.

Required proof:

- package examples;
- materials;
- finishing methods;
- process explanation.

## Segment 5: Agencies and Intermediaries

Likely needs:

- reliable production partner;
- custom execution for client projects;
- repeatable quality;
- technical advice;
- fast estimates.

Motivations:

- reduce production risk;
- satisfy their own clients;
- outsource physical production.

Objections:

- communication speed;
- quality control;
- confidentiality;
- ability to execute unusual tasks.

Website path:

Services -> production capabilities -> contact or direct brief.

Required proof:

- production capabilities;
- technology list;
- photo evidence;
- clear inquiry process.

## Cross-Segment Needs

All audiences need:

- examples of real work;
- simple inquiry flow;
- clear contact options;
- trust signals;
- material and branding explanations;
- mobile-friendly browsing;
- fast response expectation.

## Universal B2B Structure

The website should work for any qualified client by offering three navigation paths:

1. By business type:
   - restaurants and cafes;
   - hotels;
   - corporate clients;
   - retail and packaging;
   - agencies and custom projects.
2. By product category:
   - restaurant accessories;
   - hotel accessories;
   - business products;
   - packaging and souvenirs;
   - uniforms and textile products;
   - printed and branded products.
3. By production need:
   - UV printing;
   - laser cutting and engraving;
   - embossing;
   - foiling;
   - printing;
   - carpentry or custom production.

This structure avoids forcing users to understand the internal catalog before they can request help.

## Key Questions For Client Validation

- Which audience brings the most revenue?
- Which audience has the highest growth potential?
- Which audience has the highest profitability?
- Which categories create repeat orders?
- What order sizes are ideal?
- What products should not be over-promoted because of low margin or operational complexity?
- Which segments should be emphasized after market research?
